Reference dosing information

For FDA-approved Zepbound under a clinician's prescription. It must not be assumed to apply to a differently prepared product. Never change your dose based on this app.

TimingDose
Weeks 1–42.5 mg weekly (start)
After 4 weeks5 mg weekly, if prescribed
Later+2.5 mg steps, 4+ weeks apart
Maintenance5, 10 or 15 mg (15 mg max)
  • Inject once a week, same day, any time of day.
  • Missed a dose? Take it within 96 hours (4 days). Later than that, skip it and keep your usual day. Never double up.
  • Only change your injection day if the two doses are at least 72 hours (3 days) apart.
  • Rotate between belly, thigh and upper arm.
  • Use a new sterile syringe every time and follow your pharmacist's preparation and storage instructions.
  • On the pill? You may need a backup method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase.
  • Not for use during pregnancy. Talk to your doctor if you're planning one.

Emergency

Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, trouble breathing, or fainting: get help now.

📞 Call 911

Stop and call your doctor today if you have

  • Severe stomach pain that won't go away, maybe spreading to your back, with or without vomiting
  • Pain on the upper right of your belly, fever, yellow eyes or skin, or pale stools
  • Severe vomiting or diarrhea, or very little urine (dehydration)
  • Shaking, sweating, confusion or a racing heart, especially if you also take insulin or diabetes pills
  • A lump in your neck, lasting hoarseness or trouble swallowing
  • Changes in your vision
  • Low mood that is getting worse, or thoughts of hurting yourself. If you're in danger, get help right away.

Common and usually mild

Nausea, diarrhea, constipation, less appetite, heartburn, or redness where you inject. These can come back after a dose increase.

  • Eat smaller meals and stop when full.
  • Go easy on oily or very sweet food.
  • Sip water through the day.
  • Tell your doctor if it's severe or doesn't improve.
Tell your doctor before starting if you have…

A personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, pregnancy or plans for one, past pancreatitis, gallbladder or kidney disease, severe stomach problems, diabetic eye disease, and all medicines you take.

This app helps you keep track. It is not a diagnosis, prescription or dosing advice. Only use medicine prescribed and dispensed through proper clinical channels.
